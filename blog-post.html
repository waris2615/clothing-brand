The Renaissance of Classic Tailoring in Modern Fashion

Marco ELEGANTE III Creative Director

As fashion cycles continue to evolve at an unprecedented pace, we're witnessing a remarkable resurgence of classic tailoring techniques. At ELEGANTE, our master craftsmen in Milan are embracing traditional methods while incorporating contemporary aesthetics.

The Return to Craftsmanship

In an age of fast fashion and disposable trends, there's a growing appreciation for garments that are built to last. Our clients are increasingly seeking pieces that tell a story, that carry the touch of skilled hands, and that will remain relevant for decades rather than seasons.

"We see this in our clientele," says Giovanni Rossi, our master tailor with over 35 years of experience. "They're not just buying a jacket; they're investing in a companion that will age gracefully alongside them."

"True luxury is not about the label or the price—it's about the invisible hours of skilled labor that go into every seam." — Giovanni Rossi, Master Tailor

Modern Techniques, Traditional Values

While we honor traditional methods, we're not stuck in the past. Our atelier has embraced modern technologies that enhance rather than replace human craftsmanship. Computer-aided design helps us achieve precision in pattern-making, while our craftsmen still execute the delicate hand-stitching that defines our garments.

Inside our Milan atelier

The Investment Piece

Consider the cost-per-wear calculation. A €1,000 jacket worn 100 times costs €10 per wearing. A €100 jacket worn 10 times before losing its shape costs €10 per wearing. The mathematics of quality become clear when we shift our perspective from purchase price to long-term value.

Key Elements of Classic Tailoring

  • Canvas Construction: Unlike fused garments, our jackets feature hand-padded canvas that molds to your body over time.
  • Hand-Stitched Lapels: Each lapel receives hours of individual attention from our craftsmen.
  • Functional Boutonnière: A small detail that speaks to the garment's heritage.
  • Surgeon Cuffs: Working buttonholes that can actually be unbuttoned—a hallmark of traditional tailoring.

The Future of Tailoring

As we look ahead, we see a fashion landscape where technology and tradition coexist harmoniously. Virtual fitting rooms may help with initial measurements, but the final garment will always require human skill and judgment. The renaissance of classic tailoring is not a nostalgic trend—it's a return to values that have always defined true luxury.

We're proud to be part of this movement, continuing the legacy of craftsmanship that began in our Milan atelier in 1952. The future is being stitched, one careful seam at a time.
